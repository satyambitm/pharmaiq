# Model card — PharmaIQ Lite+ one-month-ahead unit forecaster

> **Synthetic demonstration.** Trained and evaluated on generated data (`synthetic_v1`). Not for real commercial or clinical decisions.

## Intended use
Demonstrate a leakage-safe forecasting workflow for monthly pharmaceutical unit sales at region × product grain, and an apples-to-apples comparison of XGBoost, Prophet and naive baselines.

**Out of scope:** real market forecasting, prescribing or targeting decisions, estimating marketing effects.

## Data
- 1,080 monthly rows (Jan 2021 – Dec 2025), 18 region × product series. See `docs/data_dictionary.md`.
- Supervised matrix: 864 rows (targets Jan 2022 – Dec 2025; the first 12 months are history for `lag_12` and 12-month rolling stats).

## Target and features
- Target: `units_sold` in month T. Forecast made at end of month T − 1 (horizon = 1 month).
- Features: lags 1, 2, 3, 6, 12; trailing 3/6/12-month mean and std; origin-month spend, 3-month trailing spend, price, competitor index, active accounts, campaign flag; target-month sin/cos; one-hot region and product.
- Never used: target-month realized spend, price, revenue, competitor index, campaign flag.

## Models
| Model | Details |
|---|---|
| Seasonal naive | ŷ(T) = units(T − 12) |
| Last-month naive | ŷ(T) = units(T − 1) |
| XGBoost 3.0.5 | `OneHotEncoder` + `XGBRegressor(max_depth=5, learning_rate=0.03, n_estimators=300, subsample=0.8, colsample_bytree=0.8, min_child_weight=3, random_state=42, n_jobs=1)` |
| Prophet 1.1.7 | One univariate model per series, refit at every origin on history < T; additive yearly seasonality, weekly/daily off, no regressors |

XGBoost parameters were selected from 8 configurations by mean WAPE over 3 expanding folds (best mean fold WAPE 8.825%, next 8.842%, a very small margin). The final model was refit on targets through Dec 2024.

## Interpretation
- Both learned models beat both baselines on 2025 overall. XGBoost did **not** automatically win: Prophet had the lowest 2025 error overall, while XGBoost was best on every backtest fold.
- Prophet improved as history grew (fold 1 had ~30 months of history; 2025 origins had 48–59). That is consistent with Prophet needing several seasonal cycles to estimate yearly seasonality.
- The seasonal naive baseline is biased low because the synthetic series grow about 7.6% a year; same-month-last-year misses the trend.
- Per region, the winner differs; North is best served by the seasonal naive baseline in 2025. A single overall WAPE hides this.
- The 0.18-percentage-point WAPE gap between XGBoost and Prophet on 216 rows is small; no significance test was run, so treat the two as roughly comparable.

## Explainability (SHAP, 2025 rows)
Top features by mean |SHAP| (units): `lag_12` 783, `lag_1` 412, `roll_mean_12` 124, `accounts_origin` 74, `roll_mean_3` 53. These are associations in this model. Lags are highly correlated, so credit can move between them; SHAP does not show what would happen if spend were changed.

## Failure modes and limitations
- Synthetic data with a known equation: models recovering it is expected and is not evidence about real markets.
- One-step-ahead only; the scores are not a 3-month guarantee. The Jan 2026 forecast is illustrative, and its revenue estimate uses an assumed price that ignores the January price increase.
- XGBoost was trained on 18 series; a new region or product would only get the one-hot "unknown" (all-zero) encoding and history features.
- No prediction intervals in v1.0.
- Prophet runtime (≈125 s for 216 refits) is roughly 200× XGBoost's.

## Reproducibility
Seed 42, `n_jobs=1`, pinned version ranges in `requirements.txt`, package versions saved in `artifacts/metrics/run_metadata.json`. The saved model is reloaded and its predictions checked against the saved holdout predictions during training.
